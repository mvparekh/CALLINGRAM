# CALLNGRAM: Model Evaluation and Perplexity Report

## 1. Experimental Protocol

- **Corpus**: AppTek Call-Center Dialogues (Primary Corpus)
- **Total Records**: 1,746 transcripts (873 customer, 873 agent) across 16 domains and 14 accents
- **Partitioning**: Transcript-level split (80% Train, 20% Held-Out Test)
  - **Training Set**: 1,396 transcripts (96,005 sentences, 16,186 unique vocabulary words)
  - **Test Set**: 350 transcripts (23,944 sentences, ~255,000 evaluated words)
  - **Random Seed**: 42 (reproducible)
  - **Data Leakage Safeguard**: Entire dialogue transcripts are assigned to either train or test. Sentences from the same dialogue are never split across partitions.

## 3. Analysis and Academic Findings

### 3.1 Unigram vs. Bigram Comparison
- **Empirical Observation**: The Unigram model yields a mean perplexity of **551.93** and median of **497.73**, while the Bigram model yields **586.42** (median **520.03**).
- **Linguistic Rationale**: While high-frequency formulaic call center collocations (*"thank you"*, *"customer service"*, *"credit card"*) have high predictive probability under bigram transitions, conversational customer dialogue contains wide vocabulary dispersion. Under Add-1 smoothing, unseen bigram context transitions carry a uniform smoothing penalty across the large vocabulary $|V| = 16,186$.

### 3.2 The Behavior of Add-1 Smoothing on Trigram Models
- **Empirical Observation**: The Trigram model demonstrates substantially elevated perplexity (**2,845.61**).
- **Mathematical Explanation**:
  1. The parameter space for trigrams scales with $|V|^2 \approx (16,186)^2 \approx 2.62 \times 10^8$ possible context pairs.
  2. In conversational dialogue, the majority of 3-word combinations in test transcripts are unseen in the training set.
  3. Under Add-1 smoothing, an unseen trigram receives probability $P(w_i \mid w_{i-2}, w_{i-1}) = \frac{1}{0 + |V|} = \frac{1}{16,186} \approx 6.18 \times 10^{-5}$.
  4. Accumulating this small probability across numerous unseen trigram contexts creates a substantial penalty on sequence log-likelihood ($\sum \ln P$), resulting in higher cross-entropy and elevated perplexity.
  5. This behavior is an established theoretical characteristic of uniform Add-1 smoothing on higher-order N-grams in computational linguistics.

### 3.3 Transcript Perplexity Distribution
- Perplexities exhibit a right-skewed distribution across service domains:
  - Highly scripted, domain-specific calls (e.g. Banking, Delivery) exhibit lower perplexities (320–420).
  - Open-ended technical troubleshooting and diverse regional accents exhibit higher perplexities (620–850).

## 5. Conclusions & Limitations

1. **Language Modeling Validity**: The N-gram models implemented in CALLNGRAM behave in exact accordance with statistical NLP theory. Add-1 smoothing provides finite, positive probability guarantees for all conversational tokens.
2. **Predictability vs. Quality**: Lower perplexity indicates mathematical sequence predictability under the learned model, but does not measure conversational quality or sentiment.
3. **Domain Adaptation**: High-order N-grams benefit from backoff (e.g., Katz backoff) or interpolation (Jelinek-Mercer) when scaling beyond bigrams in unconstrained conversational domains.
